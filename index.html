1

Baseline: maximum Narrows flexibility

3 Springdale nights · Bryce Monday · Vegas Monday

Fri: optional Valley of Fire en route to Springdale. Sat: dry-land Zion hikes and gear pickup. Sun: Narrows, with Saturday available as a weather swap. Mon: Queen’s Garden–Navajo, then Las Vegas. Tue: relaxed morning and flight.

This remains the safest plan if hiking the Narrows is the trip’s highest priority.

2

Bryce Sunday: best overall balance

Narrows Saturday · Bryce Sunday night · Vegas Monday

Fri: skip Valley of Fire or keep it very short so everyone can collect gear. Sat: Narrows. Sun: short Zion walk, drive to Bryce, overlooks and sunset. Mon: optional sunrise, Queen’s Garden–Navajo, lunch and overlooks; leave around 2:00–2:30 p.m. Utah time and reach Las Vegas around 6:00–6:30 Nevada time.

This is the richest overall itinerary, but Saturday becomes the fixed Narrows day rather than a backup option.
3

Bryce through Monday

Two unhurried Bryce days · Return toward Vegas Tuesday

Sun: transfer to Bryce for overlooks and sunset. Mon: full hiking and scenic-drive day.

A · Stay at Bryce: leave by 9:00 a.m. MDT Tuesday for the roughly 4½-hour drive to LAS. The westbound time change helps, but weather, traffic and Turo delays reduce the flight buffer.

B · Safer: drive about two hours to St. George Monday evening, then finish the drive Tuesday. This preserves a full Bryce day without putting the entire transfer on flight day.

SundayOct 11 · The Narrows

The signature river day

  • 7:00 Breakfast; board the canyon shuttle around 7:30–8:00.
  • Reach Temple of Sinawava around 8:15–8:45.
  • Walk the one-mile paved Riverside Walk, then enter the river.
  • Turn around based on cold, energy and footing—not a predetermined landmark.
  • Allow 4–6 hours total, then hot showers, dry clothes and an easy dinner.
Fall water averages 41–53°F. Check flow, flash-flood warnings and cyanobacteria conditions that morning. The Narrows closes above 150 CFS or during a flash-flood warning.

Optional Valley of Fire stop

Worth doing if everyone is happy to trade a leisurely Springdale arrival for brilliant red sandstone, petroglyphs and a very different landscape from Zion.

75

Recommended sampler

60–90 min in the park · Minimal hiking

Drive west-to-east through the park. Stop at the Beehives, climb the Atlatl Rock stairs for petroglyphs, and finish with the 0.2-mile Elephant Rock out-and-back near the east entrance.

2h

Sampler + short hike

About 2 hr · 0.7–1.8 mi

Add Mouse’s Tank for a sandy, kid-friendly petroglyph walk, or Fire Wave/Seven Wonders if the group wants a more scenic 1.8-mile loop. This pushes Springdale later.

↗

Easy finale—or skip

0.2 mi to the formation · Flexible

Elephant Rock is the simplest last stop near the east entrance. Skip the whole park if breakfast runs late, the forecast is hot, or the kids need downtime before Zion.

My lean: keep it as a weather-and-energy option and do the 60–90 minute sampler. Don’t make Fire Wave mandatory on a travel day; sandy Valley of Fire trails often feel longer than their mileage.

A fuller Bryce field guide

Six ways to experience Bryce—from an easy rim walk to a full hoodoo loop—with official details and realistic family timing.

1

Queen’s Garden + Navajo

2.9 mi · 625 ft · 2–3 hr · Moderate

Descend Queen’s Garden from Sunrise Point, climb the Two Bridges side of Navajo Loop and finish via the rim. This is the best first-time Bryce hike.

Official NPS route
½

Sunrise–Sunset Rim Trail

1.0 mi round trip · 40 ft · About 1 hr · Easy

The easiest Bryce walk: paved, nearly level and filled with amphitheater views. Turn around whenever the group is ready.

Official NPS details
◎

Inspiration Point

Minimal walking · Best late-afternoon light

Three overlook levels reveal the Silent City and a wide sweep of the amphitheater. NPS suggests arriving well before sunset for the richest light.

Official NPS details
☀

Bryce Point

Short paved approach · Sunrise panorama

One of the park’s highest-impact, lowest-effort stops, with broad views across the amphitheater and distant plateaus.

Official NPS viewpoints
≈

Mossy Cave

0.8 mi · 150 ft · About 1 hr · Easy

A streamside walk to a grotto and seasonal waterfall. The parking area is tiny; NPS discourages visits from 10 a.m.–6 p.m. when it is busiest.

Official NPS details
♟

Rainbow Point + Bristlecone Loop

1.0 mi · 200 ft · About 1 hr · 9,115 ft

The finale to the 18-mile scenic drive, combining old bristlecone pines with far-reaching views. Expect colder and windier October conditions.

Official NPS details

Bryce images courtesy of the National Park Service. Conditions and seasonal closures can change; verify current trail status before hiking.

Zion hike choices

The Watchman plus one shorter option is the strongest Saturday combination. Observation Point remains on the list, but not as the default.

1

Watchman Trail

3.3 mi · 368 ft · 2–3 hr · Moderate

Broad canyon views, begins beside the visitor center and needs no canyon shuttle.

2

Emerald Pools

1.2–3 mi · Easy–moderate

The most flexible choice. Visit Lower Pool, then continue toward Middle Pool only if everyone still has energy.

3

Canyon Overlook

1 mi · 187 ft · About 1 hr

Dramatic scenery with steps and protected drop-offs. Parking is tiny; confirm the van meets 2026 vehicle limits.

4

Pa’rus Trail

Paved · Turn around anywhere

The easiest backup and a lovely sunset walk after the Friday drive.

5

Observation Point

6.8 mi · 300 ft · 4–5 hr

Less frightening than Angels Landing, but long. Use a commercial East Mesa shuttle for the rough trailhead road.

Official details
