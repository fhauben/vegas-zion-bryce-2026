Driving the loop

Times below are wheel time in normal conditions, before meals, hikes, photo stops and October weather. Utah is one hour ahead of Nevada during this trip.

1

Las Vegas → Springdale

Direct: 2½–3 hr · Valley of Fire day: 4½–6 hr total

Direct: I-15 north through the Virgin River Gorge, then UT-9 through Hurricane to Springdale. This is the fastest and simplest route.

With Valley of Fire: leave I-15 at Exit 75, cross the park west-to-east, take NV-169 through Overton back to I-15, then continue to Springdale. Allow roughly 3¼–3¾ hours of driving plus 1–2 hours in the park.

Direct mapValley of Fire map
2

Springdale → Bryce

Through Zion: 1¾–2¼ hr · Highway 20 bypass: about 2½ hr

Scenic route: UT-9 east through the Zion–Mt. Carmel Tunnel, US-89 north and UT-12 through Red Canyon. This is the route to take if the van fits.

Large-vehicle bypass: UT-9 west, I-15 north, UT-20 east, US-89 south and UT-12 east. NPS estimates 150 miles and about 2½ hours—only around 42 minutes longer.

Bryce → Las Vegas

Fast route: 4–4½ hr · Through Zion: 4½–5+ hr

Fast route: UT-12 west, US-89 north, UT-20 west and I-15 south through Cedar City and St. George. Use this for Monday’s return.

Scenic Zion route: US-89 south and UT-9 west through the park, then I-15 south. It is prettier but repeats Zion, adds uncertainty and may not work for the van.

Measure the Turo before departure

Beginning June 7, 2026, the Zion–Mt. Carmel Highway is closed to any vehicle exceeding a single limit: 35 ft 9 in long, 7 ft 10 in wide, 11 ft 4 in high or 50,000 lb. Mirrors and fixed accessories count. A normal passenger van may fit, but the exact 12-passenger model and any roof equipment decide the route.

Official vehicle limits
  • If within every limit: take UT-9 east through the park to Bryce.
  • If over any limit: use I-15 and UT-20.
  • Do not rely on an old tunnel escort plan; the 2026 rules changed.
  • Add time for Red Canyon pullouts and possible October weather.

Optional Valley of Fire stop

Worth doing if everyone is happy to trade a leisurely Springdale arrival for brilliant red sandstone, petroglyphs and a very different landscape from Zion.

75

Recommended sampler

60–90 min in the park · Minimal hiking

Drive west-to-east through the park. Stop at the Beehives, climb the Atlatl Rock stairs for petroglyphs, and finish with the 0.2-mile Elephant Rock out-and-back near the east entrance.

2h

Sampler + short hike

About 2 hr · 0.7–1.8 mi

Add Mouse’s Tank for a sandy, kid-friendly petroglyph walk, or Fire Wave/Seven Wonders if the group wants a more scenic 1.8-mile loop. This pushes Springdale later.

↗

Easy finale—or skip

0.2 mi to the formation · Flexible

Elephant Rock is the simplest last stop near the east entrance. Skip the whole park if breakfast runs late, the forecast is hot, or the kids need downtime before Zion.

My lean: keep it as a weather-and-energy option and do the 60–90 minute sampler. Don’t make Fire Wave mandatory on a travel day; sandy Valley of Fire trails often feel longer than their mileage.

Bryce: what to do together

A first visit has two must-do pieces: walk among the hoodoos and see the amphitheater from several rim viewpoints. These complement one another rather than compete.

Must do · centerpiece

Queen’s Garden + Navajo

2.9 mi · 625 ft · 2–3 hr · Moderate

Descend Queen’s Garden from Sunrise Point, climb the Two Bridges side of Navajo Loop and return along the rim. This supplies the essential below-the-rim experience.

Official NPS routeAllTrails · 4.9 ★

It’s a magnificent and moderately easy hike. — Gerry Weiss, AllTrails

Must do · combine these

Amphitheater overlook circuit

Sunrise · Sunset · Inspiration · Bryce Point

Walk the easy one-mile Sunrise–Sunset rim section, then drive or shuttle to Inspiration and Bryce Points. Treat these as a 1½–2½ hour sightseeing circuit, not four competing hikes.

Official NPS viewpointsAllTrails · 4.7 ★

Beautiful. Easy. Solid path. Recommended for all ages. — MNC T, AllTrails

Best at golden hour

Inspiration Point

Minimal walking · Part of the overlook circuit

The widest, most layered amphitheater view. Make this the late-afternoon or early-evening finale rather than a separate activity to choose.

Official NPS details
Quick must-see stop

Bryce Point

Short paved approach · Part of the overlook circuit

Add this panoramic stop before or after Inspiration Point. It is high impact for very little walking and does not need its own block of the day.

Official NPS viewpoints
Nice to have

Mossy Cave

0.8 mi · 150 ft · About 1 hr · Easy

A pleasant streamside add-on outside the main amphitheater. Do it only if parking is easy and you have extra time; it is not essential to a first Bryce visit.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.5 ★

The stream was really nice and the waterfall was pretty! — Eliza Naumann, AllTrails

Nice to have · half day

Scenic drive + Bristlecone Loop

36 mi round trip drive · 1 mi walk · 2–3 hr total

Combine the southern overlooks, Rainbow Point and the one-mile Bristlecone Loop. Worthwhile with a second Bryce day; easy to omit on a Monday day trip.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.6 ★

It’s cooler up here, bring a jacket. — Jay Donihoo, AllTrails

Quieter alternative

Tower Bridge

3 mi · 760 ft · 2–3 hr · Moderate

Follow the southern Fairyland Loop to the Tower Bridge spur. It is similar in effort to Queen’s Garden–Navajo but quieter, so choose it as an alternate below-the-rim hike rather than stacking both.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.7 ★

This trail was practically empty! We really enjoyed it. — Sarah Becker, AllTrails

Optional big outing

Peekaboo Loop

5.5 mi · 1,560 ft · 3–4 hr · Strenuous

A spectacular, steep loop through the Wall of Windows area. This fits your mileage range but is considerably harder at Bryce’s elevation; make it the main hike of a longer Bryce day, not an add-on.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.9 ★

Epic hike, beautiful all the way round. — Jo Iacono, AllTrails

Memorable after-dark add-on

Stargazing

30–60 min · Paved viewpoints · Weather dependent

Bryce is an International Dark Sky Park. Check the calendar for a ranger telescope program, or visit Sunset, Sunrise, Inspiration or Paria View independently; bring the warmest layers and preserve night vision.

Official NPS details
First-visit sequence: Queen’s Garden–Navajo in the morning; Sunrise and Sunset Points as part of that loop; Inspiration and Bryce Points later in the day; stargaze if skies are clear. Tower Bridge is the quieter alternative to the classic loop, while Peekaboo replaces other hiking on a bigger day.

Bryce images courtesy of the National Park Service. Conditions and seasonal closures can change; verify current trail status before hiking.

Zion: what to do together

The Narrows is the signature outing. On the dry-land day, ride the canyon shuttle and collect several short landmarks in one natural sequence instead of choosing only one.

Must do · centerpiece

The Narrows + Riverside Walk

2 mi paved round trip + river distance · 4–6 hr

Ride to Temple of Sinawava, walk the Riverside Walk, then enter the river and turn around whenever cold, footing or energy says so. This is one continuous experience.

NPS safety + conditionsAllTrails · 4.9 ★

One of a kind hike…go as early as possible. — Owen R, AllTrails

Must do · combine these

Zion Canyon highlights circuit

Zion Lodge · Lower Emerald Pool · Big Bend

On Saturday, shuttle to Zion Lodge, walk to Lower Emerald Pool, then hop off at Big Bend for the Great White Throne view. Add Court of the Patriarchs if everyone is enjoying the stops.

Official NPS trailsAllTrails · 4.5 ★

The views along the trail surpass the emerald pools themselves. — Vejas Belzinskas, AllTrails

Nice to have · pair together

Watchman + Pa’rus sunset

3.3 mi hike + flexible paved stroll

Both start near the visitor center. Hike Watchman for a satisfying half-day outing, then use Pa’rus as an easy sunset walk after dinner or gear pickup.

Official NPS trailsAllTrails · 4.7 ★

Great hike in Zion for views…without taking the shuttle. — Amanda Berg Wilson, AllTrails

Nice to have

Canyon Overlook

1 mi · 187 ft · About 1 hr

Exceptional scenery per mile, but awkward with a 12-passenger van because parking is tiny. Add it while driving toward Bryce only if vehicle rules, parking and timing cooperate.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.8 ★

Kid friendly! Parking is a challenge. — Tyler Herzog, AllTrails

Easy gap-filler

Pa’rus Trail

Paved · Turn around anywhere · Best near sunset

This is Zion’s equivalent of an easy scenic stop: use it on arrival evening, after Watchman, or whenever the group has 30–60 spare minutes.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.5 ★

Great family hike! — Kindall Johnson, AllTrails

Optional big outing

Observation Point

7 mi · 702 ft · 2½–3 hr

A superb panorama but a separate half-day commitment, not a quick landmark stop. With this trip’s schedule and usual mileage, save it for a future visit unless the group actively wants the longer hike.

Official detailsAllTrails · 4.8 ★

Amazing views of the river and Angels Landing. — Eddy R, AllTrails

Easy connector

Grotto Trail

1 mi round trip · 15–45 min · Easy

This flat, shaded path connects Zion Lodge and the Grotto shuttle stops. Fold it into the Lower Emerald Pool and lodge-area circuit when the group wants another easy mile, with chances to see deer and turkeys.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.2 ★

Perfect way to warm up your legs before longer trails. — AllTrails overview

Best weather backup

Human History Museum

22-min film · Exhibits · Scenic patio

A strong arrival-day, rest-hour or bad-weather option. The museum adds context about Zion’s people and landscape, while the back patio frames West Temple and the Towers of the Virgin. Verify seasonal hours.

Official NPS details
Optional Kolob detour

Timber Creek Overlook

1.2 mi round trip · 117 ft · 30–60 min

A short, family-friendly ridge walk at the end of Kolob Canyons Road; groups of 12 are allowed. It is about an hour from Zion Canyon, so use it only with an I-15 travel day or a dedicated Kolob visit.

Official NPS detailsAllTrails · 4.7 ★

Easy for three kids all 10 and under to complete. — Ayla Rose, AllTrails

First-visit sequence: devote one day to The Narrows. On the other, combine the Zion Lodge area, Lower Emerald Pool, the Grotto Trail and Big Bend; add Watchman and a Pa’rus sunset if the group still wants more walking. Use the museum as an easy reset, and treat Canyon Overlook, Timber Creek and Observation Point as separate timing decisions.

Expected October climate

These are historical averages—not a forecast. The trip climbs from roughly 2,000 feet in Las Vegas to about 8,000 feet at Bryce, so everyone should pack for summer-like sun and freezing mornings.

Warmest

Las Vegas

Oct 8–13 normals: about 83–85°F days · 61–63°F nights

Usually warm, sunny and dry enough for short sleeves during the day. Evenings are comfortable, but a light layer helps after the Shin Lim show or at an outdoor dinner.

Pack within reach: sun hats, sunscreen, refillable water bottles and one light sweater.

NWS October climate data
Best hiking weather

Zion Canyon

October average: 79°F high · 48°F low · 1.3 in precipitation

Afternoons are often excellent for hiking, while shaded canyon mornings can feel much colder. Daily high-to-low swings can approach 30°F. Rain is not the norm, but any storm forecast matters greatly for The Narrows.

Pack within reach: hiking layers, a light puffy or fleece, rain shells and dry clothes for after the river.

NPS Zion climate
Cold-weather capable

Bryce Canyon

October average: 58°F high · 32°F low · 3 in snow

Sunny afternoons can be beautiful, but freezing nights and October snowstorms are normal possibilities at this elevation. Wind on the exposed rim can make sunrise and sunset feel substantially colder.

Pack within reach: warm pants, puffies, hats and gloves for everyone; add waterproof shoes and traction only if the forecast calls for snow or ice.

NPS Bryce weather

One carry-on strategy

Dress in removable layers rather than packing three separate wardrobes. Keep the Bryce cold-weather kit together in the van so it does not get buried beneath luggage.

  • Warm-weather hiking shirt and pants or shorts
  • Fleece or light puffy for Zion mornings
  • Warmer puffy, knit hat and gloves for Bryce
  • Light rain shell for every traveler
  • Dry socks and a complete change after The Narrows
  • Sunscreen and water at every elevation
Check twice: review the seven-day forecasts before leaving San Francisco, then check Zion river conditions and Bryce road/trail conditions again each morning. Climate tells you what is typical; a single October storm can change the plan.

Bryce Canyon City + nearby

Bryce Canyon City itself has very few hotels, so this list also includes the in-park lodge and nearby Tropic. All work for itinerary A’s Sunday night or itinerary B’s Sunday and Monday nights.

For this trip, choose Bryce Canyon City

Its proximity is especially valuable for sunset, an optional sunrise and an early Queen’s Garden–Navajo start.

Bryce Canyon City → Tropic → Panguitch

Hotel lean: Bryce Canyon Grand Hotel for practical comfort, or Stone Canyon Inn in Tropic if you want the lodging itself to feel special.

  1. Bryce Canyon City — best logistics. About 5–10 minutes from the amphitheater, with park shuttle access. Best fit for itinerary A or B. City guide
  2. Tropic — best atmosphere. Roughly 13–20 minutes away, with quieter, more memorable cabin-style lodging—especially Stone Canyon Inn. Visit Utah comparison
  3. Panguitch — best only for savings. Approximately 30 minutes away. It feels more like a real town, but the extra driving is inconvenient after sunset and before a cold October morning. NPS services guide
